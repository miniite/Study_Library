# When a pipeline deploys a new version of a log-shipper, why is it important that the old logs keep flowing during the switch?

**Difficulty:** Intermediate · **Levels:** Fresher, SDE-1/2, SDE-2/3 · [↑ Idempotent Deploy Jobs](<../../../../10_DevOps_and_Platform/Delivery/CI_CD/Idempotent_Deploy_Jobs/_index.md>)

## Answer

To understand why it's crucial to ensure that old logs continue to flow during a CI/CD pipeline deployment of a new version of a log-shipper, let's break down the problem and its implications.

TL;DR Summary

Ensuring continuous log flow during a CI/CD pipeline switch is vital because if logs are interrupted during the transition, critical information might be lost. This ensures that monitoring systems remain up-to-date with current logs while also preserving historical data for analysis.

#### Triaging the Question

The question focuses on the importance of maintaining log continuity during an update to a log-shipper component in a CI/CD pipeline. The interviewer is probing your understanding of:

Log Shipper Functionality: How the log-shipper operates and its role within the overall system.
CI/CD Pipeline Mechanics: The process of deploying new versions and how it interacts with existing services.
Impact on System Monitoring and Analysis: The consequences of losing logs during an update.

#### Assumptions
The current log-shipper is responsible for collecting, processing, and sending logs to a central storage or analysis system.
Logs are generated continuously by various systems within the application stack.
The CI/CD pipeline includes automated tests, deployment processes, and rollback capabilities.

#### First-Principles Solution

Initial Setup: Assume the log-shipper is running in an environment where logs from multiple services need to be collected and shipped. For instance, consider a microservices architecture with several services generating logs.

Deployment Process:

The new version of the log-shipper is built and tested through CI/CD pipelines.
A deployment strategy (e.g., blue-green deploy or rolling updates) is planned to minimize downtime.

Critical Requirement: Ensure that during this transition, logs generated by services are not interrupted. This means maintaining a seamless flow from the moment the new log-shipper starts receiving logs until it fully takes over.

#### Implementation
Steps:

Blue-Green Deployment: Deploy the new log-shipper in a separate environment (green) and ensure that old logs continue to be shipped through both environments.
Rolling Updates: Gradually replace instances of the old log-shipper with the new one, ensuring continuous log collection throughout the process.
Full Solution

Pre-deployment Testing:

Verify that the new version of the log-shipper can receive and process logs without issues.
Set up a blue-green deployment strategy where both versions coexist temporarily.

Deployment Strategy:

For blue-green deployment, launch the new log-shipper (green) in parallel with the old one (blue).
Ensure that all services are configured to ship logs through the new green environment.
Gradually redirect logging from the blue environment to the green one.

Monitoring and Validation:

Monitor both environments for any discrepancies or errors during the transition.
Validate that historical logs are correctly shipped to the central storage before fully decommissioning the old log-shipper (blue).

#### Closing Summary

Maintaining continuous log flow is critical during CI/CD pipeline deployments of a log-shipper because it ensures uninterrupted monitoring and analysis. This approach helps in maintaining system integrity, avoiding data loss, and ensuring that both current and historical logs are available for troubleshooting or compliance checks.
