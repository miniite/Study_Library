# What does the term 'artifact upload' mean and why might you upload a test report or build output?

**Difficulty:** Basic · **Levels:** Architect/Principal, Fresher, SDE-1/2, SDE-2/3 · [↑ CI CD Pipeline Fundamentals](<../../../../10_DevOps_and_Platform/Delivery/CI_CD/CI_CD_Pipeline_Fundamentals.md>)

> ⚠️ Practice answer, not verified. Some answers contain mistakes; when in doubt, follow the lesson.

## Answer

Artifact upload in CI/CD refers to the process of packaging and storing the results of your builds, tests, and deployments so that they can be easily accessed and used later. This is a crucial step because artifacts provide a record of what was built, when it was built, and how it performs.

#### Triage

The question is essentially asking you to explain the concept of artifact upload in CI/CD and why specific types of outputs like test reports or build results are important to upload. The interviewer is probing your understanding of the importance of maintaining a history of builds and tests for future reference and debugging purposes.

Step-by-Step Solution
Definition: An artifact is any file generated by the build process, such as executables, libraries, configuration files, or test results.
Purpose:
Traceability and Consistency: By uploading artifacts, you can ensure that every version of your software has a complete history, making it easier to reproduce builds and troubleshoot issues.
Validation: Test reports provide insights into the quality of the build. Uploading them ensures that everyone on the team has access to this information.
Reusability: Artifacts can be reused in subsequent builds or deployed environments, reducing the need for recompilation and ensuring consistency across different stages.
Detailed Explanation
Build Outputs: These include compiled code, libraries, and other necessary files. Uploading these ensures that any developer can easily set up a working environment.
Test Reports: These documents detail the outcomes of automated or manual tests. They are crucial for understanding whether the build is stable and free from issues.

#### Example

Suppose you have a project where every time a new code change is pushed, it triggers a CI/CD pipeline that compiles the source code, runs unit tests, and deploys to a staging environment. The output of this process, including the compiled binaries and test reports, would be uploaded as artifacts.

#### Common Mistakes/Follow-Ups
Not Uploading Artifacts: Failing to upload critical files can lead to issues like bugs going unnoticed or developers having to manually rebuild components.
Overlooking Test Reports: Missing out on uploading detailed test results means you might not have a clear picture of the health and quality of your builds.

#### Closing Summary

Artifact upload is essential in CI/CD as it helps maintain a reliable record of build outputs and test results. This ensures traceability, consistency, and reusability throughout the development lifecycle.
