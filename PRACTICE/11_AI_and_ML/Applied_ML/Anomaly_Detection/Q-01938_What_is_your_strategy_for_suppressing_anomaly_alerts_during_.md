## Answer

In AIOps, managing anomaly detection during deployment windows is crucial to ensure that critical alerts are not drowned out by routine changes. The goal here is to suppress false positives (alerts triggered due to deployments) while still detecting genuine anomalies that could indicate real issues. This requires a thoughtful strategy that balances system resilience with operational efficiency.

Firstly, we need to understand the context: deployment windows often introduce noise into time series data as systems go through updates, making it challenging for anomaly detection models to distinguish between regular fluctuations and actual anomalies. To address this, we'll employ a combination of techniques including model-based detection, contextual filtering, and manual overrides where necessary.

Here’s how we can implement such a strategy step-by-step:

Identify Deployment Windows: Start by defining the known deployment windows in your system. These could be predefined times or periods when deployments are scheduled to occur.
Suppression Rules Based on Time Windows: Implement rules that suppress anomaly alerts during these known time windows. This can be done through a simple check where if an alert is generated within a known window, it’s marked as "suppressed" rather than triggering immediate action.
Contextual Filtering: Enhance the model to understand contextual factors beyond just the deployment window. For example, if a spike in error rates occurs during a known update but also sees similar spikes during other times, we can flag this for further review before fully suppressing it.
Manual Overrides and Feedback Loops: Allow operators to manually mark certain alerts as true positives or negatives based on their experience. This feedback loop helps refine the suppression rules over time.

This approach ensures that critical anomalies are not missed while minimizing the noise generated by regular operations, thereby improving overall system reliability and maintainability.
