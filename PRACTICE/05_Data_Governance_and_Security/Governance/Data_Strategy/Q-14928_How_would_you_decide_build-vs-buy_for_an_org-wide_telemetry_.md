# How would you decide build-vs-buy for an org-wide telemetry data lake versus a vendor observability platform, and which governance capabilities are non-negotiable either way?

**Difficulty:** Expert · **Levels:** Architect/Principal, SDE-2/3 · [↑ Centralized vs Federated Telemetry](<../../../../05_Data_Governance_and_Security/Governance/Data_Strategy/Centralized_vs_Federated_Telemetry.md>)

> ⚠️ Practice answer, not verified. Some answers contain mistakes; when in doubt, follow the lesson.

## Answer

Deciding between building an org-wide telemetry data lake internally or using a vendor observability platform is a complex decision that involves balancing costs, customizability, control, and compliance. The core of this decision hinges on understanding your organization’s specific needs and the capabilities required for effective monitoring and analytics.

First, let's break down what we need to consider:

Scope & Scale: Determine the volume and variety of telemetry data generated by your applications and infrastructure.
Customization Needs: Assess how much you can benefit from a pre-built solution versus needing highly customized workflows.
Governance Requirements: Evaluate regulatory and internal compliance needs, as well as data privacy and security.
Resource Availability: Consider the in-house expertise required to manage a telemetry system.

Now, let’s delve into the decision-making process:

Step 1: Assessing Customization Needs

A vendor observability platform often offers pre-built workflows for common use cases. However, if your organization has unique monitoring and analytics requirements that are not covered by any available product, building an internal solution might be more suitable.

For example:

Custom Metrics: If you need to track specific metrics that aren’t provided out-of-the-box.
Complex Alerting Logic: Custom rules for alerting based on non-standard conditions.
Real-Time Processing: High-performance requirements for real-time data processing and analytics.
Step 2: Governance Considerations

Regardless of whether we build or buy, certain governance capabilities are non-negotiable:

Data Security: Ensuring that data is protected from unauthorized access.
Compliance: Meeting regulatory requirements such as GDPR, HIPAA, etc.
Audit Trails: Maintaining logs for compliance and troubleshooting purposes.
Data Quality: Ensuring the integrity and accuracy of telemetry data.
Data Retention Policies: Defining how long data is stored.
Step 3: Resource Availability

Evaluate your organization’s ability to manage a large-scale telemetry system:

In-House Expertise: Do you have the necessary skills in-house, or will you need to hire additional personnel?
Operational Support: Will you need dedicated ops teams to maintain and scale the solution?
Step 4: Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)

Calculate the TCO for both options:

Build: Initial development costs, ongoing maintenance, and operational support.
Buy: Subscription fees, potential additional customization costs.

#### Closing Summary

In summary, deciding between building an internal telemetry data lake or using a vendor observability platform involves assessing your organization's specific needs, governance requirements, resource availability, and TCO. The key non-negotiable governance capabilities include security, compliance, audit trails, data quality, and retention policies. Ultimately, the choice depends on whether you can leverage existing solutions while meeting these critical requirements.

#### Common Mistakes
& Follow-Ups
Overlooking Customization Needs: Failing to recognize unique business requirements that a vendor solution might not cover.
Neglecting Governance: Ignoring compliance and security issues that can lead to legal and financial risks.
Underestimating Maintenance Costs: Assuming an out-of-the-box platform will require minimal operational support.
