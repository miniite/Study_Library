# How do you decide retention windows: delta.deletedFileRetentionDuration vs logRetentionDuration, balancing time-travel needs against storage cost?

**Difficulty:** Deep · **Levels:** Architect/Principal, SDE-1/2, SDE-2/3 · [↑ Streaming Ingest Tuning](<../../../../TOOLS/Databricks/Databricks_Platform/Streaming_Ingest_Tuning.md>)

> ⚠️ Practice answer, not verified. Some answers contain mistakes; when in doubt, follow the lesson.

## Answer

Deciding on retention windows in Databricks involves a balance between ensuring the availability of historical data (time travel) and managing storage costs efficiently. The key settings are delta.deletedFileRetentionDuration for cleaning up deleted files and logRetentionDuration for archiving logs, which together impact how long you can use time travel features effectively.

#### Triaging the Question

The question asks about balancing two critical factors:

Time Travel Needs: This is driven by business requirements to query historical data, ensuring that operations have access to past states of their datasets.
Storage Cost Management: Managing storage costs by limiting how long data is retained can help reduce overall operational expenses.

#### Assumptions
and Context
Databricks Delta Lake uses delta.deletedFileRetentionDuration to automatically clean up old versioned files after a specified period, ensuring that only the latest versions are stored.
logRetentionDuration sets the retention policy for log files generated by Delta operations, which can help in maintaining operational visibility but also consumes storage.
Step-by-Step Solution
1. Understanding Time Travel

Time travel is crucial for scenarios like:

Audit Trails: Tracking changes over time to ensure compliance and data integrity.
Rollbacks: Easily reverting to previous states of the dataset if needed.
Historical Queries: Running queries on historical snapshots of the data.

The delta.deletedFileRetentionDuration determines how long these historical versions are retained. A shorter duration means lower storage costs but could affect your ability to perform time travel operations effectively. Conversely, a longer retention period ensures better support for time travel at the cost of increased storage usage.

2. Storage Cost Management

Storage costs can be significant in large-scale data environments. Managing these costs involves setting appropriate limits on how long data is retained:

Log Files: These contain metadata about operations and are crucial for debugging but less critical than historical data.
Deleted Files: Automating the cleanup of old versions helps manage storage more effectively.
3. Practical Considerations
Business Requirements: Align with business needs to determine how far back in history is necessary.
Operational Workloads: Assess whether frequent queries or rollbacks are common and plan accordingly.
Cost Modeling: Perform cost-benefit analysis based on the expected storage usage and query frequency.
4. Implementation

To implement an optimal retention strategy:

Set delta.deletedFileRetentionDuration: Based on your time travel needs, decide how long you need to retain historical data. For example, if business requirements demand a year of history, set this duration appropriately.
Configure logRetentionDuration: For log files, consider a shorter retention period as they are less critical for time travel but important for operational visibility. A common practice is to retain logs for 30 days or so.

#### Closing Summary

Deciding on the right retention windows involves carefully balancing time travel requirements with storage cost considerations. delta.deletedFileRetentionDuration and logRetentionDuration serve distinct purposes, and their settings should reflect your specific needs and constraints. Regularly review these policies to ensure they align with evolving business demands and operational costs.

#### Common Mistakes
and Follow-Ups
Over-Storage: Retaining too much historical data can lead to excessive storage costs without adding significant value.
Under-Retention: Failing to retain enough historical data might impact time travel capabilities, leading to compliance issues or loss of important data.
Improper Trade-offs: Balancing these settings correctly requires a thorough understanding of both the business and technical implications.
