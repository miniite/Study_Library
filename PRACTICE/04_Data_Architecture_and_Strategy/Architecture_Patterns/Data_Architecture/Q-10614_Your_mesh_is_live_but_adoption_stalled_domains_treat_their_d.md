# Your mesh is live but adoption stalled — domains treat their data products as an afterthought. Diagnose the incentive/organizational failure and design the mechanisms (metrics, funding, recognition) that fix it.

## Answer

To diagnose why adoption of our data mesh is stalled, we must first understand the underlying incentives and organizational dynamics at play. The primary issue here is likely a misalignment between the value creation process and individual or departmental incentives. A well-designed data architecture should not only facilitate data sharing but also ensure that those who contribute to the data ecosystem benefit appropriately.

#### Triage
Understanding the Problem
Identify Incentives: Departments might prioritize their immediate operational needs over contributing to a shared data pool, fearing reduced visibility or control over their unique datasets.
Organizational Culture: A culture of silos may be preventing effective collaboration and sharing.
Lack of Recognition: Data contributors might not see tangible rewards for their efforts.

#### First-Principles Solution

To address these issues, we need to design a comprehensive framework that includes:

Metrics: Quantifiable measures to track data quality, usage, and contribution.
Funding Mechanisms: Allocation of resources based on data consumption or contributions.
Recognition Programs: Formal recognition for data stewards and contributors.
Step-by-Step Solution
1. Metrics

We need to establish clear, measurable metrics that capture the value generated by data sharing:

Data Quality Metrics: Data accuracy, completeness, timeliness (e.g., Mean Time Between Errors - MTBE).
Usage Metrics: Number of queries executed, number of downstream users, and impact on business outcomes.
Contribution Metrics: Number of datasets created, updates made, and improvements in data quality.

2. Funding Mechanisms

Funding should be linked to data consumption and contributions:

Data Consumption Fund: Allocate budget based on the number of queries run against shared datasets.
Contribution Fund: Distribute rewards based on the volume and quality of contributed datasets.

3. Recognition Programs

Recognition should be formalized through awards, promotions, and public acknowledgment:

Data Steward of the Month/Year: Awarded to individuals who have significantly improved data quality.
Data Contribution Awards: Recognize departments for creating new datasets or improving existing ones.

#### Closing Summary
+ Common Mistakes/Follow-Ups

By implementing a well-defined metrics system, funding mechanisms, and recognition programs, we can align individual incentives with organizational goals. Common mistakes include underestimating the importance of culture change or failing to establish clear ownership and accountability.

def allocate_funds(queries, contributions):
