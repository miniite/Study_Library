# Probe: you keep saying 'data as a product' — but who's actually accountable when a data product's SLA breaks at 2am and the domain team is asleep? Where does the operational responsibility really land?

**Difficulty:** Deep · **Levels:** Architect/Principal, SDE-2/3 · [↑ Platform-level Data Quality](<../../../../04_Data_Architecture_and_Strategy/Platform_Strategy/Data_Platform_Strategy/Platform-level_Data_Quality.md>)

## Answer

When discussing "data as a product," it’s crucial to understand that while the phrase emphasizes treating data similarly to any other software product, accountability for its performance and reliability still needs to be clearly defined. This question probes exactly where operational responsibility lies when a critical data product's service-level agreement (SLA) is breached during off-hours. To address this, let's break it down from first principles.

Firstly, we need to understand the roles involved:

Domain Team: They own the business logic and are responsible for the domain-specific use cases.
Data Engineering Team: They build, maintain, and optimize data pipelines and infrastructure.
IT Operations Team: They ensure that the underlying IT systems are stable and reliable.

The operational responsibility for a data product's SLA should be shared among these teams in a structured manner:

Domain Team Accountability: The domain team should have a vested interest in ensuring their data is clean, relevant, and up-to-date. However, they typically do not have direct control over the underlying infrastructure or pipeline maintenance.
Data Engineering Team Accountability: This team owns the end-to-end responsibility for data pipelines, ensuring that they are robust, scalable, and performant. They should be responsible for setting up monitoring and alerting systems to notify relevant stakeholders when issues arise.
IT Operations Team Accountability: They ensure the physical infrastructure is stable and can handle the load without breaking down. They need to prioritize critical services during off-hours.

To formalize this responsibility, we often establish a governance framework that clearly outlines:

Roles and Responsibilities Matrix: Defining who does what in detail.
SLA Agreements: Clear Service Level Objectives (SLOs) and Service Level Indicators (SLIs).
Incident Management Process: A well-defined process for escalating issues, triaging them, and ensuring they are resolved promptly.

For example, a typical approach might involve:

The Data Engineering Team setting up continuous monitoring and alerting systems.
The IT Operations Team being responsible for maintaining the infrastructure during off-hours.
The Domain Team providing domain-specific context that helps in diagnosing and resolving issues more effectively.

By breaking down responsibility this way, we ensure there is no ambiguity about who is accountable when an SLA is breached. It's also crucial to have a cross-functional incident response team that includes representatives from all these teams to collaboratively address the issue.
