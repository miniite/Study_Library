# What is a monolith and why might a team keep it instead of moving to microservices?

**Difficulty:** Intermediate · **Levels:** Architect/Principal, Fresher, SDE-1, SDE-1/2, SDE-2/3 · [↑ Service Mesh](<../../../../06_Cloud_Computing/Cloud_Foundations/Cloud_Fundamentals/Service_Mesh.md>)

## Answer

In the context of cloud fundamentals, understanding what a monolith is crucial for any backend engineer or architect. A monolithic architecture involves all components of an application being tightly coupled and deployed as a single unit. This contrasts with microservices architectures, where different parts of the application are decoupled into smaller, independent services. The decision to keep a monolith over moving to microservices depends on several factors.

Firstly, let's define what constitutes a monolith: it’s essentially an application where all components are tightly integrated and deployed together, typically in one codebase. This monolithic structure makes it challenging to scale or update individual parts without affecting the whole system. However, there can be valid reasons why teams might choose to retain this architecture.
