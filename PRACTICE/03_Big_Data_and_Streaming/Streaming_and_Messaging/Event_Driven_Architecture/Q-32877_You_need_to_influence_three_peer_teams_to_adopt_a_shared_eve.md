# You need to influence three peer teams to adopt a shared event schema they didn't design and don't love. You have no authority over them. Walk me through your actual strategy to land this without a mandate, and what you'd concede to get buy-in.

**Difficulty:** Deep · **Levels:** Architect/Principal, SDE-2/3 · [↑ Breaking Schema Changes](<../../../../03_Big_Data_and_Streaming/Streaming_and_Messaging/Event_Driven_Architecture/Fat_vs_Thin_Events/Breaking_Schema_Changes.md>)

## Answer

When tasked with influencing three peer teams to adopt a shared event schema they didn't design and don’t love, my approach would be multifaceted, leveraging a combination of technical, social, and strategic elements to ensure buy-in without direct authority. The key is to foster collaboration, align interests, and provide tangible benefits.

First, I would triage the situation: identify the pain points each team has with the current schema, understand their core goals and constraints, and map these to potential improvements or enhancements that align with their objectives. By doing so, I can make a compelling case for why adopting the new schema will be beneficial in the long term.

Next, I would reason from first principles by starting with the foundational concepts of event-driven architecture (EDA) and message queues. Key points include:

The importance of decoupling: How EDA enhances system resiliency and scalability.
The benefits of a shared schema: Reduced coupling and improved interoperability across teams, leading to easier maintenance and faster development cycles.

To land the strategy, I would propose a series of steps:

Conduct a Needs Assessment: Schedule one-on-one meetings with key stakeholders from each team to understand their specific concerns and requirements.
Design Collaborative Workshops: Organize joint workshops where teams can collaboratively refine the event schema, incorporating feedback and improving its fit for all parties involved.
Demonstrate Benefits: Develop proof-of-concept implementations that showcase how adopting a shared schema can solve existing issues and unlock new capabilities.
Leverage Small Wins: Start with small, incremental changes that provide immediate value, which will build momentum and trust among the teams.
Engage Influencers: Identify and engage influential individuals within each team who are more likely to champion the change from within.

By being flexible and willing to concede on certain points, I can ensure smoother adoption:

I might agree to minor adjustments in the schema if it significantly improves the usability for a particular team.
I would be open to providing additional support or resources, such as training sessions or dedicated developer hours, to help teams transition more smoothly.

The overarching goal is to position the shared event schema not just as a technical requirement but also as a strategic enabler that aligns with each team’s broader objectives. By focusing on mutual benefits and fostering a collaborative culture, I can drive successful adoption without resorting to mandates or authority.

In conclusion, my strategy involves understanding the stakeholders' needs, demonstrating clear value, engaging influencers, and being willing to make compromises where necessary. The ultimate aim is to create a win-win situation that leverages the power of shared architecture for mutual benefit.

#### Common mistakes
to avoid include:

Failing to understand the underlying concerns and motivations of each team.
Overemphasizing technical benefits without addressing the social and organizational barriers.
Insisting on rigid changes that do not align with all teams' priorities, leading to resistance.
