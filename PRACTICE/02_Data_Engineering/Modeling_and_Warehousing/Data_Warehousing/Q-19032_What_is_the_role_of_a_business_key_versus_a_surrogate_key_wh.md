# What is the role of a business key versus a surrogate key when matching records across loads?

**Difficulty:** Intermediate · **Levels:** Architect/Principal, Fresher, SDE-1/2, SDE-2/3 · [↑ Distribution Keys](<../../../../02_Data_Engineering/Modeling_and_Warehousing/Data_Warehousing/Distribution_Keys.md>)

## Answer

In data warehousing, understanding the roles of business keys and surrogate keys is crucial for accurate record matching and efficient data management. This explanation will delve into their definitions, purposes, and practical applications at different levels of experience.

#### Triaging the Question

The question focuses on two key concepts in data warehousing: business keys and surrogate keys. The interviewer is probing your understanding of how these keys are used to ensure accurate record matching across different data loads. Business keys represent meaningful identifiers that reflect actual business entities, while surrogate keys are artificial keys introduced for technical purposes.

#### First Principles Solution
Business Keys: These are natural or composite keys derived from the core business entities (e.g., customer ID, product code). They are typically used to uniquely identify records in a star schema model and provide meaningful context.
Surrogate Keys: These are synthetic identifiers generated by the database system, often auto-incrementing integers. Surrogate keys are used for technical purposes such as joining tables efficiently.

#### Implementation
Business Key Example

Imagine you have a Sales fact table that records sales transactions:

Sales (
    SaleID (Surrogate Key),
    Date,
    Amount,
    CustomerID (Business Key),
    ProductID (Business Key)
)

Here, CustomerID and ProductID are business keys that provide meaningful identifiers for customers and products.

#### Cons
ider a DimCustomer dimension table:

PLAINTEXT
DimCustomer (
    CustomerSK (Surrogate Key),
    FirstName,
    LastName,
    Address
)

In this case, CustomerSK is the surrogate key used to join with other tables in the data warehouse.

Practical Application

When loading new data from a source system, you need to match records correctly. For instance, if there are changes or new customers being added:

Use business keys for meaningful matching and reporting.
Use surrogate keys for efficient joins and technical operations.

#### Common Mistakes
Over-reliance on Business Keys: While business keys provide context, overusing them can lead to performance issues during complex joins.
Ignoring Surrogate Keys: Relying solely on business keys without using surrogate keys can result in inefficient query execution plans.

#### Closing Summary
and Follow-Ups

In summary, the use of both business keys and surrogate keys is essential for effective data warehousing practices. Business keys provide meaningful identifiers, while surrogate keys enhance performance through efficient joins. Understanding their roles helps ensure accurate record matching and optimized query performance.

As a fresher or an intermediate Data Engineer, it's crucial to grasp these concepts thoroughly. For more advanced roles, consider how these keys interact with complex ETL processes and data lineage tracking.

    CustomerSK INT PRIMARY KEY AUTO_INCREMENT,
